🛡️Safety & Security

General Safety Tips

  • Paris is generally safe, but be aware of pickpockets in crowded areas (Metro, tourist sites)
  • Keep valuables out of sight and don't carry large amounts of cash
  • Use the official Paris Metro and licensed taxis; avoid unmarked taxis
  • Stay aware of your surroundings, especially at night
  • Keep copies of important documents (passport, ID) separate from originals
  • Share your itinerary with family back home
  • Trust your instincts—if something feels unsafe, move to a busier area

Avoiding Scams

  • Be cautious of street "jewelry" offers and fake petition scams
  • Don't accept unsolicited help with directions or travel advice from strangers
  • Use ATMs in well-lit, public areas (inside banks are safest)
  • Verify prices before ordering at cafés—check the menu board

Travel Insurance

Consider purchasing travel insurance that covers medical emergencies, lost luggage, and trip cancellations. This is especially important for international travel.

📋Documents & Legal Requirements

Passport & Visa

U.S. citizens do NOT need a visa for France for stays up to 90 days. Your passport must be valid for the duration of your stay.

What to Carry

  • Valid U.S. Passport (keep original in safe location, carry copy)
  • Travel insurance documents
  • European health insurance card (EHIC) if applicable
  • Credit cards and some cash (EUR)
  • Driver's license if you plan to rent a car
  • Airline/train confirmation numbers
  • Hotel reservation confirmations

Lost or Stolen Documents

If your passport is lost or stolen, contact the U.S. Embassy immediately at +33 (0)1 4312 2222 or visit in person at 2 Avenue Gabriel.

💶Money & Banking

Currency

France uses the Euro (EUR, €). 1 EUR ≈ $1.10 USD (rates vary).

Getting Cash & Cards

  • ATMs are widely available—withdraw EUR directly rather than exchanging at tourist rates
  • Most credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted; inform your bank of travel dates
  • American Express is less commonly accepted than in the U.S.
  • Notify your bank BEFORE traveling to avoid card blocks
  • Carry backup cards in case one is lost

Tipping

Tipping is not customary in France—service is usually included in the bill. A small tip (5-10%) is appreciated for excellent service but never expected.

🏥Health & Medical Information

Before You Go

  • Check that your vaccinations are current (especially routine ones)
  • Pack prescription medications in original containers with labels
  • Carry a list of your medications and any allergies
  • Consider travel insurance that covers medical care

If You Get Sick

  • SAMU (Medical Emergency): 15
  • Hospitals are marked with a white "H" sign
  • Pharmacies (pharmacie) are marked with a green cross—staff speak some English
  • Non-emergency care: Ask your hotel for a doctor recommendation

Useful Medications to Pack

  • Pain relievers (ibuprofen, acetaminophen)
  • Allergy medication
  • Stomach upset remedies
  • Blister treatment
  • Any personal prescription medications

🚇Transportation Tips

Paris Metro

  • Buy a carnet (10 tickets) or Navigo Easy card for discounts
  • Validate your ticket in the machine before entering the platform
  • Keep your ticket until exiting (you may be checked)
  • Avoid empty cars late at night; sit near the conductor's car

Taxis & Ride-Sharing

  • Use official white taxis with meters or ride-sharing apps (Uber, Bolt)
  • Agree on prices for unlicensed taxis before getting in
  • Taxis include a small fee for luggage

Walking

  • Paris is very walkable—wear comfortable shoes
  • Stay on marked crosswalks and obey traffic signals
  • Watch for scooters and bicycles on the pavement

📱Communication & Internet

Mobile Phone Service

  • Check international roaming rates with your U.S. provider
  • Consider purchasing a local SIM card or Europe travel plan
  • French providers: Orange, SFR, Bouygues
  • WiFi is available at most cafés, hotels, and the Metro

✨Traveler's Tips & Customs

Cultural Etiquette

  • Greet with "Bonjour" when entering shops—it's considered polite and expected
  • French people appreciate effort to speak French, even if imperfect
  • Kiss on both cheeks is a common greeting among friends
  • Avoid loud conversations in public spaces

Shopping & Business Hours

  • Most shops close for lunch (12:30-2:30 PM)
  • Many shops close on Sundays; check ahead
  • Department stores are open longer; supermarkets open earlier
  • ATMs and pharmacies have extended hours

Smart Packing Tips

  • Comfortable walking shoes are essential—Paris involves lots of walking
  • Bring a small daypack or crossbody bag
  • European electrical outlets are different (Type C/E plugs)—bring an adapter
  • Voltages in France are 230V (not 110V like U.S.)
  • Pack layers—spring weather in Paris can be unpredictable

Getting the Most from Your Trip

  • Purchase a Museum Pass for discounted access to major attractions
  • Visit major sites early in the morning to avoid crowds
  • Learn to use the Paris app for real-time transit info
  • Take time to sit at a sidewalk café and observe (people-watching is fun!)
  • Don't over-schedule—leave time to get lost and explore
  • Take photos, but also put the camera down and experience the moment

✅Pre-Departure Checklist

  • ☐ Passport is current and valid for the duration of your stay
  • ☐ Travel insurance purchased and documents saved
  • ☐ Bank and credit card companies notified of travel dates
  • ☐ Flight and hotel confirmations printed or saved digitally
  • ☐ Emergency contacts list prepared (family, embassy, insurance)
  • ☐ Vaccinations checked and up to date
  • ☐ Prescription medications packed in original containers
  • ☐ Power adapter/converter packed for electronics
  • ☐ Share itinerary with family or trusted friend
  • ☐ Make copies of important documents
  • ☐ Check weather forecast and pack appropriately
  • ☐ Download offline maps and translation apps

💫Final Thoughts

You're going to have an amazing adventure in Paris! These tips are here to help you stay safe and prepared, but remember that most travelers have wonderful experiences. Parisians are friendly (especially if you try the language), the food is incredible, and the city is absolutely beautiful.

Most importantly: Be present, stay aware, be respectful of the culture, and enjoy every moment. Paris is waiting for you! 🇫🇷
